> "When I greeted her in Korean, her shoulders dropped two inches. Suddenly, she wasn't just a 'limited\-English\-proficiency neurosurgery patient' on a hospital census—she was a halmeoni (grandmother) whose fears and pride had a voice."


In my fifth clinical blog reflection, I shared the experience of conducting a comprehensive psychosocial assessment in Korean with an elderly patient ("halmeoni") and her devoted grandson. For many older Asian immigrants, hospital stays are compounded by linguistic isolation, reluctance to "burden" family members, and systemic gaps in culturally concordant post\-acute care.


This encounter also invited deep self\-reflection on countertransference—seeing echoes of my own family and heritage in the room—and reaffirmed why my lifelong mission is to serve aging immigrant communities at the intersection of neurosurgery, gerontology, and clinical social work.
