**Original Post Link:** EP1\. Finding Hope in the Chaos (September 16, 2024\)



> "In the middle of beeping monitors, post\-operative neuro checks, and hurried morning rounds, there is a quiet sacredness in pulling up a chair and asking a patient: 'What matters most to you right now?'"


Walking onto the neurosurgery floor at Mount Sinai West for the first time as a clinical social work intern felt like stepping into a fast\-moving river. Patients arrive after sudden brain aneurysms, spinal cord compressions, or newly discovered brain tumors—events that divide a family's life into 'before' and 'after' in a single afternoon.


In those moments of acute medical chaos, I quickly realized how vital the bridge between medicine and social work truly is. While the surgical team focuses on stabilizing intracranial pressure and motor function, my role is to sit with the human aftermath: listening to patients' fears, helping overwhelmed spouses navigate crushing medical bills and insurance barriers, and translating complex emotional and social realities into structured Epic chart assessments so the entire care team sees the whole person.


Hope in a hospital doesn't always look like a miracle cure; often, it looks like making sure no vulnerable patient walks through the darkest week of their life unseen or unheard.
